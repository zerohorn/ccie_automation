3.5.f-g: Confirm telemetry arrives, then triage and fix it

Blueprint 3.5.f "Confirm data transmission" and 3.5.g "Identify network issues and make changes", under 3.5 "Create YANG model-driven telemetry subscriptions". Exam release v1.1.

Mental snapshot

  • A telemetry subscription fails quietly. Nobody sees a red light, they see an empty graph. 3.5.f is the skill of proving data moved; 3.5.g is finding the first hop where it stopped and changing the right object.
  • "Valid" means the device accepted the subscription. It says nothing about whether a session to the receiver exists or whether a single update was sent.
  • Subscription state and receiver state are two separate things, each with its own show output. An XPath typo kills the first; a closed port or wrong VRF stalls the second.
  • Work a ladder from the device outward and stop at the first rung that fails: process, subscription, receiver connection, device counters, wire, collector, database, dashboard.
  • Counters are cumulative. After a fix, look for counters that move (connection count, transmit count), not for old error counts to disappear.
  • IOS XE receivers cannot be edited in place on the documented path, so "make changes" often means delete and recreate. NX-OS disables a receiver after 20 errors and needs it unconfigured and reconfigured.
  • In gNMI a quiet open stream is legal. The end of the initial dump is signalled by sync_response, not by silence.

Where this fits

Fundamentals to exam depth

L1: Concept

Pull telemetry (SNMP polling) fails loudly: the poll times out and the NMS alarms. Push telemetry fails by absence. The device decides when to send, so a dead pipeline looks identical to a quiet network until someone notices a flat graph. That is why the blueprint separates "build the subscription" from "confirm data transmission".

Think of three planes. Configuration plane: the subscription exists and the device accepted it. Session plane: a transport session to the receiver is up (dial-out) or a client session exists (dial-in). Data plane: payloads are being emitted, received, decoded, stored and queried. A fault in one plane can look healthy in the plane before it, so you need evidence per plane.

L2: Mechanics

RFC 8639 defines a receiver state machine for configured subscriptions, shown below. A receiver starts in connecting, moves to active once the subscription-started notification is delivered, and only active receivers get event records. If transport cannot be established it falls to disconnected. It can go to suspended when the publisher hits a buffer or CPU limit while the transport stays up, and returns to active after a subscription-resumed or subscription-modified notification. For dynamic subscriptions the publisher never sends subscription-started.

Datastore subscriptions (RFC 8641) add their own failure vocabulary. A push-update carries a complete filtered snapshot (periodic updates and the initial sync of on-change). A push-change-update carries only changes. If the publisher cannot honour its obligations it sets incomplete-update on the record or suspends the subscription. Section 5 lists suspension reasons period-unsupported, update-too-big and sync-too-big. See RFC 8641✅. On the exam you will meet the Cisco CLI words, not these identities, but the identities explain what the device is protecting itself from.

L3: Exam detail, the evidence ladder

IOS XE (guide 17.5, lab runs 17.16)

The 17.5 Model-Driven Telemetry chapter Programmability Configuration Guide, Cisco IOS XE Bengaluru 17.5.x - Model-Driven Telemetry✅ gives the monitoring commands: show telemetry ietf subscription all, ... dynamic brief, and ... <id> detail. Detail shows ID, State, Stream, Encoding, Filter type and XPath, Update policy and Notes. The same data is in the Cisco-IOS-XE-mdt-oper model, via NETCONF get with an empty mdt-subscriptions filter or a RESTCONF GET. There the subscription state reads sub-state-valid, the type is static or dynamic, and each receiver has a state such as rcvr-state-connecting or rcvr-state-connected with a comment like "Connection retries in progress". The NETCONF sample also shows updates-in, updates-dampened and updates-dropped. The chapter documents no sent counter, so do not tell yourself "transmit count" exists on IOS XE.

The TAC note Configure Model-driven Telemetry on Cisco IOS-XE Devices with YANG Suite✅ (tested on 17.3.3) adds show telemetry internal connection, with peer address, port, VRF, source address, transport, state and profile, and two states: Active and Connecting. It also gives the two classic cases. Valid but nothing arrives: look for Connecting, ping the receiver, test the port from a host (connection refused means closed or unreachable), and on VRF devices set the source VRF and source address. Invalid: read Notes; the example is an XPath with an extra slash after the module prefix.

Three limits from the 17.5 chapter shape your fixes. Only the first receiver is tried (configured subscriptions use later ones only if the first is unavailable). Receivers cannot be modified; to change one you delete and recreate. A disconnected receiver on a valid subscription can be nudged by rewriting it with identical characteristics. Configured subscriptions go away only with no telemetry ietf subscription or a configuration delete; dynamic ones can be removed with clear telemetry ietf subscription <id> or the kill-subscription RPC (both from 16.11.1). After a switchover, dial-out reconnects by itself while dial-in subscriptions must be recreated.

One trap: a periodic update whose filter selects nothing is still delivered, as an empty datastore-contents-xml element. A flowing but empty stream points at R1 (the XPath) or at missing data, not at transport.

NX-OS (guide 9.3(x), lab N9Kv 9.3(8))

The 9.3 chapter Model Driven Telemetry✅ is richer than IOS XE here. Transport: show telemetry transport (status Connected, Disconnected or Idle), then show telemetry transport <id> stats (connection and disconnect counts, transmit count, last TX time) and ... errors (connection and Tx error counts, last Tx error and return code). Collection: show telemetry data collector brief and details, show telemetry control database, show telemetry control stats. Traces: show system internal telemetry trace and show tech-support telemetry. The device disables a gRPC receiver after it sends 20 errors (wrong certificate, timeouts); recover by unconfiguring and reconfiguring the receiver IP under the destination group.

The newer TAC note Configure and Verify Telemetry on Nexus Platform✅ (NX-OS 10.6(4), newer than the 9.3(8) pin) gives a clean order: transport Connected, collector brief, control stats for the skip reason, transport errors, show telemetry config errors (shows a configured VRF against the correct VRF), then stats to confirm recovery. Its example failure is Last Tx Return Code UNAVAILABLE with skipped collections rising. It also states that collection counters are cumulative.

gNMI dial-in

The client is the receiver, so R2 is your own Subscribe call. The spec gNMI specification✅ says that after the initial updates the target sends sync_response: true. With updates_only the first message is the sync_response. Paths that are valid but do not exist yet produce nothing and the RPC stays open. Valid but unimplemented paths return UNIMPLEMENTED and syntactically invalid ones INVALID_ARGUMENT. The IOS XE 17.5 gNMI chapter gNMI Protocol✅ documents show gnxi state as its only show command, and sample errors: a bad xpath arrives as TERMINATED, an unsupported encoding as UNIMPLEMENTED, an empty result as NOT_FOUND.

Collector side

For the lab chain, R5 and R6 are outside Cisco's guides. Grafana's InfluxDB troubleshooting page Troubleshoot InfluxDB data source issues✅ separates connection errors (URL needs http://, default port 8086, timeouts) from "datasource is working. 0 measurements found" (connection fine, nothing written). For empty results check the time range and measurement and field names. A raw InfluxQL query needs WHERE $timeFilter, per the InfluxDB query editor✅ page.

Micro-drill (about 10 min): break it, find it, fix it

Target: Cat8000V (IOS XE 17.16) pushing to the TIG stack on HORNLAB01. Assumes the 3.5.d dial-out subscription exists. The tracker says that has not been pointed at TIG yet, so build it first if needed.

  1. Requirement: produce one piece of evidence per rung R0 to R3 for subscription 100, written in a text file. Include state, receiver state, and the filter in effect.
  2. Fault A: add an extra "/" after the module prefix in the XPath. Identify it using only a show command. Fix it.
  3. Fault B: change the receiver port to one the collector does not listen on. Identify using the connection output and one host-side test. Fix it.
  4. Fault C: stop the Telegraf container. Decide which rung fails first and which show output does not change.

Reference names: subscription State, Notes, Filter, receiver address and port, source VRF, source address, connection state Active/Connecting.

Verify: after each fix the state returns to Valid and Active, and Grafana Explore shows a new point inside the last minute.

Hint

Fault A shows up at R1, in Notes. Fault B shows at R2 as Connecting while R1 stays Valid. Fault C leaves R1 and probably R2 looking odd or healthy depending on the receiver; compare what moves in the oper counters with what Grafana shows. Since receivers cannot be modified, expect to remove and recreate for B.

Solution outline

A: show telemetry ietf subscription 100 detail shows Invalid and a parse error in Notes; remove with no telemetry ietf subscription 100 and recreate with a correct XPath. B: show telemetry internal connection shows Connecting; the host test to the wrong port is refused; recreate the receiver with the right port. C: the device cannot know the collector application died if the TCP session still opens; R0 to R3 may look fine and R4 (tcpdump) or R5 (container down) is where the fault appears. Recall check

Today

  1. Subscription says Valid and show telemetry internal connection says Connecting. Name three checks and the VRF-related settings.
    AnswerPing the receiver, test the receiver port from a host (refused means closed or unreachable), check firewalls and the configured port number; set source VRF and source address on VRF-enabled devices (TAC note 217427).
  2. Where do you find why a subscription is Invalid, and what was the cause in the Cisco example?
    AnswerNotes field in show telemetry ietf subscription <id> detail. An extra "/" after the module prefix colon in the XPath produced a parse error.
  3. A gNMI SAMPLE stream stays open and a path never produces data. Is that an error, and what message ends the initial dump?
    AnswerNot necessarily: valid paths that do not exist yet send nothing and the RPC stays open. The initial dump ends with sync_response: true. Invalid paths return INVALID_ARGUMENT, unimplemented ones UNIMPLEMENTED.
  4. NX-OS: Status Connected, Last Tx Return Code UNAVAILABLE, skipped collections rising. Meaning, and why not trust old counters after the fix?
    AnswerReceiver unreachable or application stopped. Counters are cumulative, so confirm recovery by connection count rising, queued size 0 and dropped 0. Also remember 20 receiver errors disables the connection until the receiver IP is unconfigured and reconfigured.
